# The Caretaker

James Leyland Kirby. Ambient/hauntological project named after Jack Torrance's role in Kubrick's *The Shining* — the caretaker who has "always been the caretaker."

Fisher returns to The Caretaker repeatedly as the pre-eminent sonic theorist of hauntology. The project's trajectory — from nostalgic memory to amnesia — maps exactly onto what Fisher sees as the cultural condition of the post-End-of-History moment.

## Fisher's key arguments

### Crackle as the audio-correlate of hauntology

> "The great sonic-theoretical contribution of The Caretaker to the discourse of hauntology was his understanding that the nostalgia mode has to do not with memories but with a memory disorder."

The early releases (*A Stairway to the Stars*, *Memories from the Haunted Ballroom*) stage crackle not as decoration but as the sound of temporal dislocation itself — "the audio-correlate of hauntology." Crackle veils the past while making the dimension of time audible: the time-wound, dyschronia, the sense that "time is out of joint."

### From nostalgia to amnesia

The project's later development moves from the honeyed appeal of a lost past toward something darker: anterograde amnesia — not the inability to remember, but the incapacity to make new memories. The inability to distinguish present from past. Fisher reads this as "the cultural pathology of a clipshow culture locked into endless rewind."

### Nostalgia mode vs. hauntological preoccupation

Fisher distinguishes The Caretaker from Jameson's "nostalgia mode." The nostalgia mode brackets out history to present itself as new (indie's mock-Tudor). The Caretaker foregrounds temporality — the very act of remembering and its failure — which is what makes the work hauntological rather than merely nostalgic.

## Key albums (discussed by Fisher)

- *Memories from the Haunted Ballroom* (1999) — Gold Room reconstructions; "suggestive audio-fog"
- *A Stairway to the Stars* (1998) — Al Bowlly, prewar ballroom croon under crackle
- *Persistent Repetition of Phrases* (2008) — shift toward amnesia and neurological disorder
